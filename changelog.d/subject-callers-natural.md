### Fixed — `--for` prints the callers of the one function a sentence asks about

- `--for`: a sentence that names one function and asks what depends on it now gets that function's `<callers by="subject">` block, as the short subject already did. Examples: "I want to add a new parameter to transformNodes; what would that affect?", "who calls async_prepare_setup_platform and would break if its signature changed?", "I need to change how decompress_data works; what depends on it?". The plain-word limit now leaves out the task frame: question leads, pronouns, helpers ("I need to"), conjunctions and prepositions. The sentence also needs a dependents cue (change, add, affected, breaks, depends, calls, used, update, modify, invokes, references, rename, exercise), read in its dependents sense, and exactly one named function. A sentence without one reads as before, for example "how does X work", "where is X defined" or "how do A and B relate". So does a cue that asks what the function itself does ("what does X depend on", "what does X modify"), a noun or purpose ("API calls", "used to"), a negated cue ("I changed nothing in X"), and a sentence naming two functions ("when X changes, who calls Y?"). This change does not alter the route: only a short descriptive subject can take the name-exact route, through the existing flip checks, and the rest of the answer is byte-identical. Gate: `test/subjectanchorcheck.sh` arms SC.

Answers, against main with the same arguments: of the 60 practice questions, the stored wording is unchanged (26 Complete, 118 items); the first natural wording goes from 25 to 27 Complete and 114 to 118 items, the second from 20 to 22 Complete and 105 to 108 items, and no question loses a tier or an item. Every question that moved gains the new block. Of 321 in-sample calls, 316 print the same answer as main (timestamps aside) and 5 that already printed the block gain its legend clause "(several: the first with callers)".
