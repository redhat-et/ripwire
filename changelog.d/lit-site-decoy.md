### Changed — `<lit>` orders a literal's sites by nearness to the answer and cuts the far ones when they do not all fit

The literal-site block used to list a literal's byte-exact sites in an order that did not look at the question: on a widely used name (a setting read in hundreds of places) its 32 rows filled with readers in unrelated directories, CI scripts and other tables that hold the same string. Now each read site is placed against the answer above the block: its signature rows and the bodies it prints (the fold, the slot, `--detail` and the auto bodies), plus the literal's own source definitions (a symbol named its last segment that the earlier segments place; a generic last segment alone, such as `enabled`, anchors nothing). A site is near when it stands in one of their files or directories, or its function calls or is called by one of them; a hub (called from more than 16 functions, such as `getSetting`) puts no caller one call away, and when two or more of the answer's rows are themselves readers of the literal (a body match) those rows anchor nothing. Table rows still come first, and near sites come before far ones in each group.

A block whose sites all fit is only reordered (a vendored, minified or generated copy is listed after the product sites); nothing is cut. When the sites do not all fit the rows: a vendored, minified or generated copy beside a product site is cut (an authored `static/` or `locale/` file is product code); when something stands near the answer, the far sites are cut; when a table row stands in the answer's file or one call from it, the rows of the other tables are cut; with none there and several tables, the far rows outside the home table are cut. The home table is the file with the most table rows of the literal, then the one that declares it (an X-macro `#define` list over a map or set), then the one with the most sites outside any function; a tie names no home and cuts nothing. The registrations the reserve keeps (quoted keys outside any function) are never cut. When the cut moved a row (a site the old order would have listed is gone), `lit_cut="N"` counts the sites cut and a legend clause, printed only with it, says why; a cut of sites that stood past the rows anyway is not said again (`capped="1"` and `shown=` already say it). `next=` still lists every site. A block with nothing near the answer and a single table keeps its old rows. Gate: `test/forliteralcheck.sh` arms D1-D14.
