### Changed — `<lit>` orders a literal's sites by nearness to the answer and cuts the far ones when they do not all fit

The literal-site block used to list a literal's byte-exact sites in an order that did not look at the question: on a widely used name (a setting read in hundreds of places) its 32 rows filled with readers in unrelated directories, CI scripts and other tables that hold the same string. Now each read site is placed against the answer above the block: its signature rows and the bodies it serves, plus the literal's own source definitions. A site is near when it stands in one of their files or directories, or its function calls or is called by one of them; table rows still come first, and near sites come before far ones in each group. When the sites do not all fit the rows and something stands near the answer, the far sites are cut; so are the rows of a table far from it when a table row stands near it, or, with no table row near and several tables holding the literal, the rows outside the table file that holds most of its sites. A CI, tool, vendored or generated copy (a dot directory, a vendoring directory, the demo/generated tier) is never a row beside a product site. `lit_cut="N"` counts the sites cut, a legend clause (printed only with it) says why, and `next=` still lists every site. A block whose sites all fit is only reordered; a block with nothing near the answer and a single table keeps its old rows. Gate: `test/forliteralcheck.sh` arms D1-D7.
