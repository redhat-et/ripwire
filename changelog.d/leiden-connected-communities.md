### Changed — every `--communities`, `--zoom` and `--partition` module is one connected piece, and says so

Louvain's local moving can pull a bridge symbol out of a module and leave the rest of it in two pieces that share no call edge (Traag, Waltman & van Eck, Sci Rep 2019, "From Louvain to Leiden"). Measured on the unchanged algorithm, the level-0 partition had 0 such modules on this repo, 2 on envoy, 7 on vscode and 49 on llvm-project; the coarser `--zoom` levels had 1 more on envoy, 6 on vscode and 7 on llvm-project. The local-moving core now ends with a connectivity step: each module is split into its connected pieces (a union-find over the module's own edges). The step runs at every `--zoom` level too, so a module at any level is connected over symbols. Splitting a module whose pieces share no edge always raises modularity, so the step never trades quality for the guarantee. When nothing is split, the partition and its module ids are byte-identical to before. This is the connectivity guarantee of Leiden's refinement step, not the whole step: Leiden also rebuilds each module from singletons, which repairs badly connected modules too, and that part is not implemented.

Every `<communities>`, `<zoom>` and `<ctx-partitions>` root (and the `--partition` JSON) carries `disconnected=N`: the modules whose members are not one connected piece of the call graph, counted by a separate pass over the symbol graph (on `--zoom`, summed over every level). It reads 0 unless the split failed. `--zoom --mermaid` carries the same count as a `%% disconnected=` comment. Default output changes only where a module was split: on vscode, 43 symbols move out of 7 modules; on llvm-project, 1,810 symbols out of 49. On this repo's own code nothing moves. New gate `test/leidencheck.sh`, on the fixture `test/leidenfix/graph.c`, a 15-function graph on which plain local moving leaves one module in two pieces.
