### Changed — a `--for` answer prints deep same-file sibling rows as tail pointers

A ranked `<sigs>` `<d>` row whose file a higher `<d>` row already shows, ranked past the 5th `<d>` row and placed after the first 10 answer rows, is now printed in its place as a tail pointer `<t p="FILE" sym="NAME" r="RANK"/>` (its ranker order kept): still on screen, no longer an answer row. On the graded sets these rows are where most picked decoys sat (a same-file neighbour of the right row, deep in the list, beside a right item already printed). The first 10 answer rows are never touched, so nothing moves up into them.

A row stays a row when it carries `mem=` (a type with its member list), when it is a `<c>` callee under a `<h>` hop row, when it is `Owner.member` / `Owner::member` with its `Owner` row among the four `<d>` rows above, when the index holds a call path of at most two proven edges (no name-only binding, no link) between it and one of the first three resolved answer rows, or when a kept row's `same_as=` names it. A short legend comment rides before the first pointer. A token budget leaves the answer as shaped, and `est_tokens=` is re-priced over the removed bytes. CLI `--for` and MCP `for` share the one renderer; `RIPWIRE_NO_SIBLING_DEMOTE=1` turns it off.

The rule and its first three guards come from the picked-census and SIBLING-DEMOTE guard studies; the call-path guard comes from ROW-FEATURES-2. `test/forsiblingdemotecheck.sh` pins every condition and guard, each with a twin.
