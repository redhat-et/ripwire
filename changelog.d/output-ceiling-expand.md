### Fixed — a bare `--expand` answer fits an agent's screen; what did not fit is named, never dropped

A bare `--expand=NAME` served bodies up to a 64 KB budget, and the budget counted only body text — not the `<b>` tags' `sibs=` / `inc=` lists. On GA1 DEV, `--expand=toString` on gson (33 definitions) printed 51,406 characters and Newtonsoft.Json's hub 34,773: past Claude Code's ~30,000-character inline limit, so the agent got a file path and a 2 KB preview.

A bare `--expand` now budgets its bodies at 16 KB and, when the `<bodies>` section still comes out over 20,000 bytes, re-renders at a proportionally smaller budget, so the whole answer stays under the 24,000-character output ceiling (`src/outputceiling.h`). Nothing new is invented to say what was cut — the existing vocabulary carries it: a cut body says `truncated="1" lines="lo-hi/T"` and its `next=` is the slice that continues it (`--expand=FILE:LINE:NAME:A-B`), and definitions that did not fit are counted by `unserved_total=` and listed by `unserved_next=` (the first 16, as before). By default a file over the ceiling budget is no longer a whole-file candidate, and `reason=` says so. An explicit `--pack-budget-bytes=N` is your size and is honoured as before; `--for` is unchanged.

Measured on GA1 DEV (11 repos, one tree each, the map's first three named symbols): largest `--expand=<hub>` answer 51,406 → 18,845 characters; answers over 30,000: 2 → 0. Gate: `test/outputceilingcheck.sh` (arms E1–E6: a 1,400-line body's slices rebuild it exactly; 40 definitions are served or named, none dropped).
