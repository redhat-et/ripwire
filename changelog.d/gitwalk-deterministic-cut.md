### Fixed — the wall-clock history budget no longer decides a number, so two runs really are byte-identical

A git history walk is bounded by a commit count (`RIPWIRE_GIT_WALK_COMMITS`, 200,000) and by a wall clock (`RIPWIRE_GIT_WALK_SECONDS`, 20 s). The two cuts were served the same way — keep the prefix, label the numbers floors — but only one of them is reproducible. A commit cut is exactly the newest N commits, the same ones on any machine. A clock cut is wherever the machine happened to be when the clock ran out, so the Windows packaging job, which runs one unzipped exe twice and requires byte-identical output, saw two runs cut at 9,756 and 9,770 commits: `churn=` and `amp=` were absent from one run's rows and present in the other, and on a 3,000-commit fixture a clock cut decided whether `--owners` answered at all or refused. That is a determinism-contract violation, not a flake, and it would equally have reached captures, goldens, agent caches and the cross-platform diff.

**A clock-cut walk's commits are now not counted at all.** Every consumer — `--for`/`--metrics`' `churn=` and `amp=`, `--cochange`, `--hotspots`, `--rank-by=churn` and `churn-decay`, `--owners`, `--quality-delta`'s short-horizon churn — drops what it read and takes the no-history degrade it already had, each of which was already disclosed: `churn=` absent and `amp=` callers-only under `history_unread="1"`, a uniform churn prior, a refusal that names the budget. The disclosure names no commit count for a clock cut, because that count *is* the load-dependent number; it says the commits are not counted, that an absent `churn=` is not zero churn, and that `RIPWIRE_GIT_WALK_COMMITS` bounds the walk by a commit count every machine reproduces. A clock cut is also never cached any more (it has nothing to cache), while a commit cut is still stored and still served as a floor with its count. `--pr-context`'s `git diff` reads already worked this way ("a prefix of the rows is not a list"); the history walk now matches them.

A **commit** cut is unchanged in every respect. The qchurn cache moves to scheme 7, so no blob written under the old rule is read.

The determinism claim loses the exception the old behaviour forced on it: `--help=all`, `README.md` and `docs/REFERENCE_GUIDE.md` say byte-identical run-to-run again, with nothing after the comma. New arms in `test/historywalkcheck.sh` (D) force the divergence deterministically — a git shim that hands the walk exactly 0, 40 and 90 records and then stalls past the budget — and require one answer, byte for byte on stdout and stderr, from all three; the pre-fix binary differs on that pair, which is arm (R3).
