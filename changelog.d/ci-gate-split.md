### Changed — CI: `binoverridecheck` and `mcpframehonestycheck` never share a shard, and `binoverridecheck` dispatches longest-first

Both gates ran at their 1200 s budget on the macos-26 Release leg's shard 2/4 (1,137.4 s and 1,106.2 s on #754's run; 1,207.8 s and 1,201.3 s, killed, on #756's) and failed at random on unrelated PRs. The cost-balanced slicer put them in the same shard on every leg. On the macos-26 plain leg, whose skip list drops `binoverridecheck`, `mcpframehonestycheck` finished in under 262 s.

- `test/pargates.py` has a declared anti-affinity table, `SHARD_APART`. The slicer never hands a gate a shard that already holds another member of its group, as long as there are at least as many shards as members. The pair is the table's only group. `--shard-plan` now lists each shard's gates, as its comment always said it did.
- `test/binoverridecheck.sh` runs its sub-gates longest-first, using `.github/binoverride-weights.json` (869 rows: the larger of two timed local runs, staged and unstaged, 2026-11-26). A gate with no row runs first, as unknown. Its output and verdicts are unchanged. Replayed against the measured durations, the 6-worker wall drops from 313.7 s to 276.5 s on the staged run (the CI shape) and from 333.4 s to 303.4 s on the unstaged one.
- New gate `test/shardapartcheck.sh`. It reads the shard counts and skip lists from the workflow and checks that, for each of them and for N = 2..8, the real slicer's plan partitions the gate list and the pair lands in different shards. A synthetic root with the table emptied puts the pair in one shard, which shows the check can see a collision.
